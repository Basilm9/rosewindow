2. Introduction

2.1 Purpose of the SRS

This document specifies the requirements for Rose Window, a single-player stained-glass puzzle game that runs in a web browser. It states what the system does, not how it is built, and it is written so that every requirement can be demonstrated to have been met.

The intended readers are the course instructor, acting as the customer; the members of Group 18, acting as the development organization; and any engineer who later maintains the system. It serves as the contract between those parties concerning the product’s behavior and its required quality attributes.

Revision 1.1 extends the specification to cover the progression, persistence, and challenge capabilities added since revision 1.0, and corrects a statement in §5.2 that the product had outgrown. Where a capability is deliberately excluded it is recorded as such in §5.2 rather than omitted silently, so that the boundary of the contract is unambiguous. Revision 1.2 adds the requirements of the mobile overhaul and rewords FR-1.5 and FR-12.2 to match the build. Revision 1.3 adds the safety properties of §4.6, a reliability measure (NFR-1.8), and time limits on the restore delay and the response to a tap (NFR-3.6, NFR-3.7). Revision 1.4 labels the scenario that opens each task in §3 and adds the acceptance tests of Appendix C.

2.2 Problem statement

Tile-laying and dice-drafting puzzle games ask a player to satisfy several competing constraints at once. Their appeal comes from the tension between a placement that is legal, a placement that scores well now, and a placement that preserves options later. That tension is difficult to deliver in software because the rules must be enforced exactly, the reason for a rejected move must be communicated immediately, and the consequences of a move must be visible before it is committed.

Existing implementations of this genre typically enforce rules correctly but explain them poorly. A player who attempts an illegal move is told only that it is illegal. The player must then reconstruct the rule from memory, which converts a puzzle about planning into a puzzle about recalling the rulebook.

Rose Window addresses this by making rule feedback immediate, specific, and located at the point of the attempted action. Every rejected placement identifies which law was broken and, where applicable, which existing dice caused the conflict. A legality preview shows the outcome of a placement before it is committed.

The game adds a scoring mechanism of its own. After each round a beam of light enters the window at an announced edge and refracts through the placed glass according to the color of each die it strikes. The path the beam takes is a consequence of placements made several rounds earlier, so the player must reason about a spatial mechanism that unfolds over time rather than optimizing each round in isolation.

A single run is short. To give a reason to return, the system keeps a record of play across runs, sets a shared daily puzzle, and lets a player hand a specific puzzle to someone else. These are secondary to the puzzle itself, and the requirements in §3.13 through §3.16 are written so that none of them can affect how a run is scored.

The system must therefore satisfy four demands: enforce a non-trivial rule set exactly; explain each enforcement decision well enough that a player learns the rules by playing; make a run reproducible, so that a given starting condition always produces the same game and any defect can be reproduced on demand; and keep a record of play that never becomes a way to influence a score.

2.4 Document conventions

Functional requirements are numbered FR-n.m and grouped in §3 by the user task they serve. Non-functional requirements are numbered NFR-n.m and grouped in §4 by quality attribute. Numbers are stable: a requirement that is withdrawn in a later revision retains its identifier, marked as withdrawn, rather than being reused. For the same reason the tasks added in revision 1.1 appear at §3.11 and after, rather than being inserted where they would sit in play order.

The word "shall" denotes a binding requirement. "Should" denotes a preference that is not binding. No requirement in this document uses "may" to state system behavior; permissive statements appear only in prose.

A requirement marked Deferred states a capability the product is contracted to provide and whose rules are implemented and tested, but which the interface does not yet expose. Such requirements are identified in the verification column and are not claimed to be met.

Every requirement carries a verification method stating how it will be demonstrated to have been met. Four methods are used: Test, meaning an automated test asserts the behavior; Demonstration, meaning the behavior is exhibited on a running system during acceptance; Inspection, meaning the property is confirmed by examining the product or its documentation; and Analysis, meaning the property follows from an argument over the design. A requirement that could not be assigned one was rewritten until it could.

3.5 Task: illuminate the window

Scenario. At the end of each round a beam enters the window at an edge announced in advance and travels through it, bending as it strikes colored glass and awarding points for each die it passes through. The entry point is announced before the round is played so that the player can place with the coming beam in mind.

The beam is the part of the system with the most intricate behavior, and the requirements below fix it precisely, including the two cases where the rule as originally stated was ambiguous.

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ID        Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                                            Verification method
  --------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------
  FR-5.1    The system shall announce the entry point of the coming beam before the round is played, stating the cell and the direction of travel.                                                                                 Demonstration; Inspection of Figure S-5.

  FR-5.2    The system shall admit the beam at the announced cell, travelling in the announced direction.                                                                                                                          Test.

  FR-5.3    The system shall award, for each cell the beam enters, the value of the die in that cell multiplied by the multiplier then in effect. A cell holding no die shall award nothing.                                       Test — cell-by-cell assertions over hand-built windows.

  FR-5.4    The system shall turn the beam ninety degrees clockwise when it strikes a red or yellow die, ninety degrees counterclockwise when it strikes a blue or green die and shall not turn it when it strikes a purple die.   Test — one case per color.

  FR-5.5    The system shall award the points for a die before applying that die’s own refraction, so that a die which bends the beam is scored at the multiplier in effect before the bend.                                       Test — a case constructed to distinguish the two orderings.

  FR-5.6    The system shall increase the multiplier by one each time the beam is turned to a ceiling of five.                                                                                                                     Test — a path with more than four bends confirms the ceiling.

  FR-5.7    The system shall prevent the beam from turning again for a number of cells equal to one less than the value of the die that caused the previous turn. Dice within that interval shall still be scored.                 Test — cases at value 1, at value 6, and in between.

  FR-5.11   The system shall add the points awarded by each round’s beam to a running total for the run.                                                                                                                           Test — the per-round sequence sums to the reported beam total.

3.6 Task: resolve a deadlocked round

Scenario. The placement laws can produce a position in which no die available to the player has any legal cell. Without explicit handling the run would stall. The system detects this and resolves it in the player’s favor: the round is forfeited, but the window is still illuminated and still scores.

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ID       Requirement                                                                                                                                                                        Verification method
  -------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  FR-6.1   The system shall determine, before requiring a placement, whether any die available to the player has at least one legal placement. A die held in hand shall count as available.   Test — hand-built deadlocked windows, including one where only the held die fits.

  FR-6.2   The system shall forfeit a round in which no available die has a legal placement, returning the unplaced dice to the bag.                                                          Test.

  FR-6.3   The system shall refuse to forfeit a round in which a legal placement remains available.                                                                                           Test — forfeiting a solvable position raises the defined condition.

  FR-6.4   The system shall illuminate and score a forfeited round exactly as it would a played round.                                                                                        Test — a forfeited round contributes a beam score.

  FR-6.5   The system shall inform the player that a round was forfeited and why.                                                                                                             Demonstration.

  FR-6.6   The system shall complete all eight rounds of a run regardless of how many rounds are forfeited.                                                                                   Test — every seed in a sampled range completes.

Scenario. A run takes long enough that a player will sometimes close the page mid-window. The system restores an interrupted run on the player’s next visit. It does so by replaying the actions the player took rather than by restoring a saved board, so that stored data can never put the game into a state the rules would not permit.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ID        Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                       Verification method
  --------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  FR-12.1   The system shall record the player’s actions as a run proceeds.                                                                                                                                   Test; Inspection of the stored record.

  FR-12.2   The system shall restore an interrupted run automatically, without asking, when the player returns.                                                                                               Demonstration — begin a run, close the page, and reopens it.

  FR-12.3   The system shall reconstruct a restored run by replaying the recorded actions through the same rules that governed them, and shall not restore a stored board, score, or bag contents directly.   Test — a restored run reproduces the interrupted run exactly; Inspection.

  FR-12.4   The system shall reject a stored record that is malformed, out of range, or records an action the rules do not admit, and shall begin a new run instead.                                          Test — one case per rejection condition, including an oversized action list and an out-of-range position.

  FR-12.5   The system shall discard the stored record when a run is completed or abandoned.                                                                                                                  Test; Demonstration.

  FR-12.6   The system shall remain fully playable when storage is unavailable or refused, offering no restoration rather than failing.                                                                       Demonstration in a browser with storage disabled.

3.13 Task: accumulate a playing record

Scenario. A single run is short, and a player who finishes one has no way of knowing whether it was a good result. The profile answers that: it records what has been played, marks best, and grants experience and shards. It is held entirely on the player’s own device.

The requirements below are written so that nothing in the profile can affect the scoring of a run. Progression rewards appearance only, as §3.14 states.

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ID         Requirement                                                                                                                                                                         Verification method
  ---------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  FR-13.1    The system shall record each completed run once, retaining its seed, window pattern, mode, final score, beam total, and completion time.                                            Test — recording the same run twice leaves the profile unchanged.

4. Non-functional Requirements

The qualities below are stated as separately verifiable requirements. Where a quality is commonly stated as an unmeasurable adjective, it has been replaced by a property that can be demonstrated.

4.1 Reliability

Reliability here means that the system produces the same result from the same starting condition, and that its rule evaluation is correct on every path, not merely on the paths a tester happened to try.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ID        Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                       Verification method
  --------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  NFR-1.1   The system shall produce an identical run (identical patterns, objectives, dice, entry points, and scores) for any two runs begun with the same seed.                                             Test — a frozen golden-master run is compared against the stored expected result on every change.

  NFR-1.2   The system shall consume randomness only from a single seeded source, so that no run depends on ambient state.                                                                                    Inspection — the source is the only randomness in the product; Analysis.

  NFR-1.3   The system shall reject malformed window pattern data at load time rather than during play.                                                                                                       Test — malformed definitions raise an error on parse.

  NFR-1.4   The beam traversal shall terminate for every window state and every entry point.                                                                                                                  Analysis — each cell may be entered at most once per direction, bounding the traversal at four times the cell count; Test.

  NFR-1.5   The complete automated test suite shall pass before any change is accepted into the main line.                                                                                                    Inspection of the continuous integration configuration and its history.

  NFR-1.6   The system shall validate stored profile and run data before using it and shall fall back to a clean profile or a new run rather than failing when the data cannot be read or does not conform.   Test — corrupt, truncated, and out-of-range stored data cases.

  NFR-1.7   A restored run shall reproduce the interrupted run exactly, in board state, score, and remaining resources.                                                                                       Test — an interrupted and restored run is compared against the uninterrupted equivalent.

  NFR-1.8   Across 1,000 automated runs on seeds 1 to 1,000, the system shall complete every run with zero failures: no crash, no stalled run, and no score mismatch on replay.                               Test — the automated player plays and replays seeds 1 to 1,000. Zero failures bound the failure rate below 0.3 % a run at 95 % confidence (rule of three).
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.2 Robustness

Robustness means that no action available to the player, legal or otherwise, and no data the system reads back from storage, can leave it in an inconsistent state.

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ID        Requirement                                                                                                                                                                 Verification method
  --------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------
  NFR-2.1   No rejected player action shall alter the contents of the window, the draft pool, the bag, or the score.                                                                    Test — state is compared before and after each rejection case.

  NFR-2.2   Every rejection shall carry a machine-readable reason distinguishing it from every other rejection.                                                                         Test — one case per reason; Inspection.

  NFR-2.3   The system shall reject an action requested in a phase that does not permit it, without altering state.                                                                     Test — illegal actions are attempted in each phase.

  NFR-2.4   The system shall treat a request to place outside the bounds of the window as a defect in the caller and shall fail loudly rather than silently ignoring it.                Test.

  NFR-2.5   The system shall complete a run from every seed in a sampled range without stalling.                                                                                        Test — an automated player completes runs across a range of seeds.

  NFR-2.6   The system shall treat all stored and externally supplied data (restored runs, profiles, and challenge codes) as untrusted and shall admit none of it without validation.   Test — hand-crafted invalid inputs for each; Inspection.

  NFR-2.7   The system shall bound the size of any stored record it will accept, so that an oversized record is rejected rather than processed.                                         Test — an over-length action list is refused.

  NFR-3.2   The system shall present the legality of a candidate placement within one display frame of the pointer entering the cell.   Demonstration; Analysis — the evaluation is a bounded pure computation over sixteen cells.

  NFR-3.3   The system shall become playable within three seconds of a first request over a broadband connection.                       Demonstration — measured load of the built product.

5. Design and Implementation Constraints

5.1 Standards compliance

The following standards bind the product or this document.

-   IEEE Std 830-1998, Recommended Practice for Software Requirements Specifications, governs the form and content of this document.

-   ECMAScript 2022 is the language level targeted by the product. The product is written in a statically typed superset and shall type-check with no errors under its strictest setting.

-   The W3C Accessible Rich Internet Applications specification governs how the window, the draft pool, and the controls are exposed to assistive technology, as required by NFR-5.3 and NFR-5.4.

-   The CSS reduced-motion media feature governs the suppression of animation required by NFR-5.6.

-   Coordinated Universal Time, as defined by ISO 8601 calendar dates, governs the determination of the current day required by FR-15.3.

-   The product shall run in current versions of browsers implementing the above; no support is required for browsers that do not.

5.2 Development constraints

The following constraints are imposed by the course, by the deployment target, or by decisions already taken and recorded here so that they bind subsequent work.

-   The product shall run entirely in the browser. It shall have no server component and no account system, and shall require no network access once loaded, as required by NFR-3.5.

-   The product shall be deployable as static files, with no runtime infrastructure.

-   The product shall hold all player data on the player’s own device: the run in progress, recorded as the player’s actions and replayed on restoration; and the player profile, comprising experience, shards, bests, run history, achievements, palette selection, and daily progress. No player data shall be transmitted from the device.

-   Because there is no server, a score reported through a shared challenge is self-reported. The product shall not represent such a score as verified and shall not present a challenge code’s check value as authentication, as required by FR-16.6.

-   The product shall not employ a game engine or a continuous rendering surface. Its discrete, turn-based nature makes these unnecessary, and avoiding them keeps the interface inspectable by automated tooling, which NFR-4.5 depends on.

-   Development is AI-assisted, as required by the course. Conventions that agents must follow are recorded in the repository and are treated as part of the build infrastructure rather than as documentation.

-   No change shall reach the main line without passing the automated verification described in NFR-4.5.

-   The tier thresholds of FR-8.4, and the reward and unlock amounts of §3.13 through §3.15, are provisional. They are tunable quantities under NFR-4.4 and are expected to change following playtesting; changes to them are not changes to this specification.

-   Multiplayer in any synchronous form, leaderboards, and any ranking that would require an authoritative score are outside the scope of this specification.
